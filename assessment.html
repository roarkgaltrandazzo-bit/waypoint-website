Advancement Readiness Self-Check

This self-check helps you see whether your fundraising activity has enough advancement structure behind it. Answer the questions first, then share where we should send your results.

How scoring works

Use the answer that best reflects your current reality.

  • Not in place = 0
  • Inconsistent = 1
  • Partly in place = 2
  • Clearly in place = 3
What this measures

Donor records, stewardship, board participation, campaign planning, event follow-up, lead measures, annual rhythm, recurring giving, and dependency risk.

1. Donor records are current, usable, and owned by someone.
2. We know who gave, who stopped giving, who increased giving, and who needs follow-up.
3. First-time donors receive timely and personal stewardship after they give.
4. Donors hear from us between asks, not only when we need money.
5. Campaigns and appeals are planned early enough to prepare lists, messages, board involvement, and follow-up.
6. Events have a clear purpose: cultivation, solicitation, stewardship, or a defined mix.
7. Gala or event attendees, sponsors, and first-time donors receive intentional follow-up after the event.
8. Board members have clear advancement roles they can realistically carry.
9. We track lead measures such as donor meetings, thank-you calls, introductions, follow-up, and stewardship touches.
10. We have an annual advancement rhythm that includes cultivation, solicitation, stewardship, data review, and planning.
11. Recurring giving or ongoing support is intentionally invited and stewarded.
12. Advancement does not depend too heavily on one person’s memory, urgency, or personal relationships.
