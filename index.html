The Funding Problem

Funding gets harder when the work behind it is scattered.

A donor gives and gets thanked, but the next meaningful touchpoint is unclear. A board member wants to help, but the role has not been defined. An event raises money, but sponsor and donor follow-up does not always carry into the next campaign.

Waypoint helps connect the work behind funding so cultivation, solicitation, and stewardship support each other.

Donor Follow-Up

Follow-up happens inconsistently.

A donor gives, receives a thank-you, and may not hear much until the next appeal or event. Stewardship needs a rhythm beyond the first acknowledgment.

Board Participation

Board members need specific roles.

Most board members are more useful when the ask is clear. Introduce someone, thank someone, attend a donor conversation, support a sponsor, or share a specific story.

Campaign Timing

Events and appeals need to connect.

A gala, annual fund, sponsorship push, and year-end appeal can all work separately and still miss the larger opportunity. The donor list, message, follow-up, and next ask need to connect.

Records and Ownership

Donor and sponsor information needs ownership.

Some information is in the CRM. Some is in email. Some is in spreadsheets. Some is known only by one staff member or volunteer. That makes the next step harder to see and easier to miss.

Advancement Framework

Advancement is a system, not a single fundraising push.

A healthy advancement system connects three kinds of work: cultivation, solicitation, and stewardship.

Before the Ask

Cultivation

Cultivation is the work before the ask. It includes donor communication, sponsor awareness, board introductions, mission storytelling, and the trust-building that prepares people to give.

The Ask

Solicitation

Solicitation is the ask itself. It includes annual appeals, event asks, sponsorship requests, giving days, campaign conversations, and major gift invitations.

After the Gift

Stewardship

Stewardship is what happens after the gift. It includes thank-yous, impact updates, reporting, personal follow-up, board appreciation calls, and communication that shows donors their gift was noticed and used well.

When those three pieces are disconnected, funding depends too much on urgency, memory, and the next event. Waypoint helps connect the pieces so the funding work has a rhythm.

Questions

Common questions before the first call.

Is the Advancement Review really free?

Yes. The first call is free. We’ll look at how your funding work is handled now and whether there’s a useful next step.

Who should be on the call?

Usually the executive director, head of school, advancement leader, development director, board president, board treasurer, or the person carrying the most fundraising responsibility.

Do we need a development director or CRM?

No. Some organizations have staff and software. Others are working from spreadsheets, email, and volunteer memory. Waypoint starts with how the work is actually being handled.

How is this different from a campaign consultant?

Campaign consultants usually focus on a specific campaign. Waypoint focuses on the advancement system that supports cultivation, solicitation, stewardship, donor communication, board participation, and sponsorship follow-up.

Do you replace our development director or make asks for us?

No. Waypoint does not replace staff or solicit gifts on your behalf. We help organize the system so your staff, board, and volunteers can work with more clarity.

How long does an engagement usually take?

A focused project may run 60 to 90 days. Larger advancement system work or advisory support takes longer. Scope is defined after the first conversation.

Is this only for schools?

No. Waypoint works with nonprofits, private schools, and mission-driven organizations that need clearer structure behind funding, donor communication, stewardship, board participation, and sponsorships.
