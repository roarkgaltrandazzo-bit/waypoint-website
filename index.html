The problem

Most mission-driven organizations do not lack people who care. They lack a clear advancement system.

Schools, nonprofits, and faith-based organizations are often surrounded by people who believe in the mission. Families care. Donors care. Board members care. Staff care. Volunteers care.

But care alone does not create a reliable funding rhythm.

  • Campaigns get planned too late.
  • Gala follow-up waits until everyone has recovered.
  • Donors receive a receipt and a thank-you, then hear little until the next ask.
  • Board members want to help, but their role is unclear.
  • Staff know stewardship matters, but no one owns the calendar.
Why Waypoint is different

Founded by an educator who understands the inside of mission-driven work.

Sarina Randazzo has spent her career in education and service. She began as a U.S. Peace Corps teacher in the Philippines before returning to the United States, where she served as a private school teacher, a founding teacher at a charter school in inner-city Milwaukee, a private school principal, and a board member for a 501(c)(3).

That experience shapes how Waypoint works. Advancement cannot be dropped onto a school or nonprofit like a generic sales process. The work has to respect the mission, the people, the board, the families, the donors, and the limited capacity inside the organization.

What changes

What changes when advancement has a system

Communication gets consistent

Families, donors, sponsors, and supporters hear from the organization between asks.

Stewardship happens sooner

Follow-up after gifts, events, and sponsorships becomes part of the plan instead of a loose end.

Board roles get clearer

Board members understand how they can help without being handed vague fundraising pressure.

Campaigns start earlier

Appeals, galas, and year-end work are planned before the deadline starts making decisions.

Tracking becomes visible

Open commitments, next steps, donor touches, and stewardship tasks stop living in memory.

The rhythm can be handed off

The work becomes easier for staff, boards, and volunteers to carry over time.

FAQ

Common questions

Who do you work with?

Waypoint works with private schools, nonprofits, and faith-based organizations that rely on donor trust, campaigns, events, sponsorships, board support, and consistent communication.

Do you only work with schools?

No. Sarina’s background gives Waypoint deep credibility in education, but the model also applies to nonprofits and faith-based organizations with similar funding challenges.

What does an engagement look like?

Most engagements begin with an assessment, then move into system design, calendar planning, campaign support, stewardship structure, board and staff enablement, implementation support, and handoff.

Do you help with galas and year-end campaigns?

Yes. Waypoint helps with campaign planning, messaging, donor segmentation, board support, sponsor follow-up, post-event stewardship, and impact communication.

Do you replace our volunteers or staff?

No. Waypoint supports and equips your people. Your organization keeps the relationships. We help make the work clearer and easier to manage.

What does this cost?

Pricing depends on scope. A focused assessment can be used as a lower-friction starting point. Ongoing support can be structured monthly based on needs, calendar, and capacity.
